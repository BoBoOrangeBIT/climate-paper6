## C01 Heat-health and adaptation models represent adaptation almost only as air conditioning
- barreca2016adapting — US temperature–mortality decline 1900–2004 attributed mainly to residential AC diffusion; threshold-type AC adaptation.
- deschenes2011climate — annual weather fluctuations, mortality and energy expenditure; AC as the adaptation margin.
- carleton2022valuing — global mortality damage function with income and climate-driven adaptation; adaptation costs priced through AC-type responses.
- chua2023net — AC prevalence lowers heat-related mortality in Japanese cities; net effect after urban heat feedback.
- colelli2020cooling — review of cooling demand in integrated assessment models; cooling is modelled as AC with threshold and setpoint.
- biardeau2020heat — maps global AC need from heat exposure; AC framed as the adaptation.
- davis2021air — AC ownership rises with income; large unmet demand in hot low-income countries.
- falchetta2024inequalities — residential cooling energy inequality to 2050; up to 4 billion people may lack AC in 2050 (notes).
- pavanello2021air — AC adaptation cooling deficit in emerging economies; 64–100 million families cannot satisfy comfort demand (notes).
- andrijevic2021future — cooling gap projections under SSPs.
- sera2020air — multi-country longitudinal study: higher AC prevalence reduces heat-related mortality.
- deschenes2022impact — benefits and costs of adaptation to climate change mortality in the US (journal version of NBER WP 30282).

## C02 Fans and direct evaporative coolers are what households actually own, at a fraction of AC energy
- iea2018future — 2.3 billion residential fans in use, more than 80 TWh in 2016, about 1.5 % of residential electricity (notes).
- agarwal2026spatializing — India 2022 household ownership: evaporative cooler only 18 %, AC plus EC 3 %, AC only 4 %; 178 vs 56 kWh per capita per year (notes).
- jay2021reducing — Lancet series: personal cooling strategies to green cities; fans first.
- malik2022potential — fan-first with AC only above the adaptive comfort limit cuts AC electricity about 76 % (5592 to 1344 GWh) (notes).
- ravanelli2016electric — fan 55–100 W vs AC 1500–5000 W, about 50-fold lower (notes).
- jay2019fanning — fanning as a sustainable alternative to AC for indoor occupational heat stress.
- unep2023global — Global Cooling Watch 2023: cooling demand, emissions and access.
- naderi2020simulation — residential direct evaporative coolers across four Iranian climate regions.
- noor2020evaporative — evaporative cooling options for Multan, Pakistan.
- falchetta2024inequalities, davis2021air — see C01 (cooling access inequality).

## C03 Fan health evidence is physiological and contested; evaporative coolers nearly absent from health literature
- jay2015should — biophysical argument that fans can help above 35 °C depending on humidity; institutional thresholds range 32.3 °C at 35 % RH to about 37 °C (notes).
- morris2019effects — crossover trial: fans beneficial at 40 °C/51 % RH, harmful at 47 °C/15 % RH.
- morris2021electric — biophysical model: fans beneficial below 39 °C (young), 38 °C (older), 37 °C (anticholinergic); beneficial on 30.0/31 hot days (96.6 %) for young adults (notes).
- tartarini2021application — Gagge model: 0.8 m/s raises critical operative temperature about 14 °C for RH above 22 %; 93 of 115 cities benefit (notes).
- foster2021quantifying — fans ineffective and potentially harmful at ≥ 35 °C when RH < 50 % (notes).
- meade2024critical — CONTRA: critical review still advises against fans above 35 °C, especially ≥ 65 years (notes).
- oconnor2024effect — CONTRA: older adults, fans at 38 °C/60 % RH gave minimal benefit, at 45 °C/15 % RH raised core temperature about 0.3 °C (notes, second-hand).
- chaseling2025thermal — older adults fan trial, thermal and perceptual responses in humid vs dry heat.
- brode2023temperature — wind lowers strain below 35 °C and in humid heat above 35 °C, but amplifies strain in hot-dry conditions.
- gupta2012electric — CONTRA: Cochrane review found no eligible trials; evidence neither supports nor refutes fans (notes).
- ravanelli2015heart — humidity-ramp trial at 36 and 42 °C; fans mitigate heart-rate rise except in very dry heat.
- hospers2020electric — fans as stay-at-home cooling; heat-index thresholds (HI 56 °C humid beneficial, HI 46 °C arid harmful) (notes).
- kenny2024indoor — review of indoor overheating and strategies, including fans and ambient cooling.
- who2024heat — WHO fact sheet: set AC to 27 °C and turn on a fan; room feels 4 °C cooler; up to 70 % cooling electricity saved.
- rugani2026creating — only recent chamber study of evaporative cooler effectiveness for thermal refuges (building science, not health).
- tewari2019field — field comfort in evaporative-cooled offices, composite climate of India.
- pavanello2025adaptation — India: evaporative coolers give reduced protection compared with AC in heat-mortality model (notes).

## C04 Gap: SET-based equivalent temperature exists in building science but is not connected to the threshold-and-setpoint form
- ashrae2020thermal — ASHRAE 55 elevated air speed method: comfort zone shifted by SET-equivalence; 0.8 m/s without and 1.2 m/s with occupant control.
- arens2009moving — explains the ASHRAE 55 elevated air speed method and the SET basis of the cooling effect.
- gagge1986standard — origin of the two-node model and SET.
- schiavon2008energy — SET-based cooling effect of air movement; energy saving from raised setpoints.
- hoyt2015extending — extending setpoints from 22.2 to 25 °C saves about 29 % cooling energy (outline); fans enable higher setpoints.
- zhou2023effects — elevated air speed in hot-humid climate extends the summer comfort zone (about 3 K per ASHRAE allowance).
- zhai2013comfort — personally controlled air movement keeps comfort in warm humid conditions (up to 30 °C, 80 % RH).
- zhang2015review — review of corrective power of personal comfort systems, including fans, in non-neutral environments.
- dedear2002thermal — adaptive comfort basis of ASHRAE 55 (used by Malik 2022 for the AC switch-on limit).

- baldwin2023humidity — debate: why epidemiology misses humidity effects physiology predicts.

- johnston2022bioaerosol — evaporative coolers raise indoor humidity and bioaerosol exposure.

- graham2025electric — CONTRA: fan effects under dehydration in extreme heat and humidity.
