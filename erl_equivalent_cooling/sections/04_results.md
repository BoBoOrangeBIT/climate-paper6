# Results

## 3.1 On the common scale, a fan is worth about 3 K and an evaporative cooler is worth the wet-bulb depression discounted by humidity

<!-- C12 -->
Figure 1(a) shows the fan ΔT at 1.2 m/s across the outdoor temperature and humidity grid. The surface is flat. At 50 % RH the fan is worth 3.6 K <!-- R:K010 --> at 30 °C, 3.5 K <!-- R:K011 --> at 35 °C and 3.3 K <!-- R:K012 --> at 38 °C, the edge of the primary operating window. Humidity matters more than temperature. At 35 °C the fan delivers 4.1 K <!-- R:K013 --> at 20 % RH but 2.6 K <!-- R:K014 --> at 80 % RH, because the two-node model credits air speed mainly through evaporative heat loss. Over 26 to 38 °C and all humidities ΔT stays between 0.7 K <!-- R:K017 --> and 4.4 K <!-- R:K018 -->. Air speed scales the surface almost uniformly (figure 1(d)), from 3.0 K <!-- R:K015 --> at 0.8 m/s to 4.1 K <!-- R:K016 --> at 2.0 m/s for 35 °C and 50 % RH. The SET cooling effect never turns negative inside the window, whereas the core-temperature variant reaches −1.5 K <!-- R:K019 --> at 45 °C and 15 % RH, which is why the main analysis applies the external 38 °C window of Morris et al [@morris2021electric].

<!-- C13 -->
The direct evaporative cooler follows the wet-bulb depression discounted by the humidity it adds (figure 1(b)). At 35 °C and 20 % RH the supply air is 11.8 K <!-- R:K026a --> cooler in dry-bulb terms and the equivalent reduction after the humidity penalty is 11.2 K <!-- R:K020 -->, with a supply RH of 66 % <!-- R:K025 -->. At the same temperature the value falls to 3.8 K <!-- R:K021 --> at 50 % RH, where the supply air reaches 81 % <!-- R:K025a --> RH and leaves the window, and to 0.7 K <!-- R:K022 --> at 80 % RH. The largest value is 13.1 K <!-- R:K023 --> at 40 °C and 15 % RH. Only 1.3 % <!-- R:K026 --> of grid cells have ΔT at or below zero, so the window is set by the supply humidity cap rather than by the sign of ΔT. Figure 1(c) places both technologies beside the air-conditioning rules, whose ΔT grows linearly with outdoor temperature, while the fan contributes a nearly constant 3 to 4 K and the evaporative cooler crosses the air-conditioning line only at low humidity. The SET-consistent air-conditioning variant gives 11.9 K <!-- R:K027 --> at 35 °C and 50 % RH, close to the nominal 12 K. Applying the same conversion to the room state measured by Rugani et al [@rugani2026creating] gives 0.6 K <!-- R:K006 --> at still air, 2.4 K <!-- R:K007 --> at 0.3 m/s and 3.7 K <!-- R:K008 --> at 0.5 m/s, which brackets the manikin cooling effect of about 3 K once the airstream at the occupant is included.

## 3.2 The fan window covers almost all hours in which air conditioning would run, and fans deliver a large fraction of its exposure reduction

<!-- C14 -->
In ERA5 2020, air conditioning under the Default rule would run for at least 100 hours per year on 76 % <!-- R:K030 --> of land area, for an area-weighted mean of 2870 h <!-- R:K031 --> per land point with a mean ΔT when on of 5.6 K <!-- R:K038 --> (figure 2(a)). Of those hours 95.8 % <!-- R:K032 --> fall below 38 °C and inside the primary fan window (figure 2(b)). Among land points with at least 100 air-conditioning hours, 81 % <!-- R:K034 --> of the area has coverage of 95 % or more, and the losses are confined to hot deserts, since only 35 % <!-- R:K101 --> of land sees any hour above 38 °C. Inside its window the fan delivers a mean ΔT of 3.6 K <!-- R:K001 -->, giving a global fan equivalence ratio of 60.6 % <!-- R:K033 --> (figure 2(c)). The point-level distribution is wide, with an area-weighted median of 82 % <!-- R:K035 --> and a 10th to 90th percentile range of 42 % <!-- R:K036 --> to 147 % <!-- R:K037 -->. Ratios above 100 % occur where air conditioning runs mostly just above 23 °C, so the fan removes more than the small excess. Under the DLS rule air conditioning runs for 1884 h <!-- R:K040 -->, fan coverage is 93.6 % <!-- R:K041 --> and the equivalence ratio rises to 69.6 % <!-- R:K042 --> because the higher setpoint shrinks the air-conditioning denominator more than the fan numerator.

<!-- C15 -->
Evaporative coolers split the world by humidity (figure 2(d)). Globally they are inside their window during 42.5 % <!-- R:K050 --> of Default air-conditioning hours, yet with a mean ΔT of 9.6 K <!-- R:K057 --> they reach an equivalence ratio of 72.8 % <!-- R:K051 -->. The spatial pattern is bimodal. Among land points with at least 100 air-conditioning hours, 44 % <!-- R:K052 --> of the area has a ratio at or above 100 % and 22 % <!-- R:K053 --> has a ratio below 25 %, with a 10th to 90th percentile range from 5 % <!-- R:K061 --> to 178 % <!-- R:K062 -->. The dry class, 68 % <!-- R:K054b --> of the area with meaningful air-conditioning demand, has an evaporative ratio of 112 % <!-- R:K054 --> against a fan ratio of 56 % <!-- R:K054a -->. The humid class, 10 % <!-- R:K055b --> of that area, reverses the ranking with 9 % <!-- R:K055 --> for the evaporative cooler and 72 % <!-- R:K055a --> for the fan. The intermediate class sits between with 45 % <!-- R:K056 --> and 63 % <!-- R:K056a -->. Under the DLS rule the global evaporative ratio rises to 110.8 % <!-- R:K058 -->, because the hours removed by the higher setpoint are disproportionately mild and humid.

## 3.3 Raising the air-conditioning threshold by 3 K with a fan avoids a large share of air-conditioning hours at no exposure cost

<!-- C16 -->
The fan plus air-conditioning combination raises threshold and setpoint by 3 K, less than the fan ΔT of 3.4 K <!-- R:K076 --> at the 26 °C and 55 % RH boundary, so exposure inside the fan window is never higher than under air conditioning alone. Globally the combination avoids 34.4 % <!-- R:K070 --> of Default air-conditioning hours and 44.1 % <!-- R:K071 --> of cooling degree-hours, while the exposure degree-hours removed reach 118 % <!-- R:K072 --> of air conditioning alone because the fan keeps running above the raised setpoint. Under the DLS rule the avoided shares are 41.1 % <!-- R:K073 --> of hours and 49.0 % <!-- R:K074 --> of cooling degree-hours. The avoided share varies little by humidity class, from 32 % <!-- R:K077 --> in the dry class to 37 % <!-- R:K078 --> in the humid class, because it depends on the hours between 23 and 26 °C rather than on humidity. Figure 3(a) and (b) and table 2 give the city results. In the humid cities Singapore, Lagos and Miami the fan window covers every air-conditioning hour, the fan ratio is 74 to 80 % and the evaporative ratio is at or below 5 %. In Phoenix and Riyadh, 1015 h <!-- R:K209 --> and 1527 h <!-- R:K219 --> exceed 38 °C, fan coverage falls to 78 % <!-- R:K201 --> and 74 % <!-- R:K211 -->, and the evaporative cooler exceeds air conditioning. Delhi and Karachi keep high fan coverage but evaporative ratios below 25 %, because their hot hours are dry while their air-conditioning hours as a whole are humid. The Default avoided share ranges from 12 % <!-- R:K204 --> in Phoenix to 57 % <!-- R:K314 --> in Paris, and under the DLS rule reaches 64 % <!-- R:K288 --> in Lagos.

## 3.4 Sensitivity

<!-- C17 -->
The global fan result is robust to the operating window (figure 3(c)). Thresholds of 35, 37 or 39 °C move the Default equivalence ratio from 60.6 % <!-- R:K033 --> to 56.6 % <!-- R:K080 -->, 59.5 % <!-- R:K081 --> and 61.6 % <!-- R:K082 -->, with coverage of 90.2 % <!-- R:K097 -->, 94.3 % <!-- R:K099a --> and 97.0 % <!-- R:K099 -->. The Foster rule gives 56.7 % <!-- R:K083 --> with coverage of 90.3 % <!-- R:K098 -->. Removing the window raises the ratio only to 64.0 % <!-- R:K085 --> and allowing negative ΔT outside the window lowers it only to 60.4 % <!-- R:K086 -->, because hours above 38 °C are few. The core-temperature variant without any window is the one assumption that changes the picture, giving 28.0 % <!-- R:K087 -->, because it credits the fan with much less cooling at all temperatures. Air speed matters more than the window, with 50.3 % <!-- R:K088 --> at 0.8 m/s and 72.4 % <!-- R:K089 --> at 2.0 m/s. The evaporative result is more sensitive to the humidity cap than to the pad. A supply RH cap of 70 % lowers the Default ratio from 72.8 % <!-- R:K051 --> to 48.4 % <!-- R:K090 -->, no cap raises it to 95.9 % <!-- R:K091 -->, and a wet-bulb rule of 24 °C gives 87.4 % <!-- R:K092 -->. Saturation effectiveness of 0.6 and 0.85 gives 64.9 % <!-- R:K093 --> and 54.3 % <!-- R:K094 -->, where the higher effectiveness lowers the ratio because more hours breach the humidity cap. Crediting a 0.5 m/s airstream at the occupant raises the ratio to 92.7 % <!-- R:K095 -->. The SET-consistent air-conditioning variant changes the denominator by less than 1 % (ratio 99.4 % <!-- R:K096 -->), so the equivalence ratios are insensitive to how the humidity setpoint is treated.
