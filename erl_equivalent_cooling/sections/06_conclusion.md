# Conclusion

<!-- C40 -->
Electric fans and direct evaporative coolers can be written in the same form as air conditioning, namely an equivalent temperature reduction applied inside an operating window, so that one exposure–response function prices all three technologies without modification. Under the Default rule, a 1.2 m/s fan operates in 96 % <!-- R:K002 --> of the hours in which air conditioning would run over global land in 2020 and removes 61 % <!-- R:K003 --> of the exposure degree-hours that air conditioning removes. A direct evaporative cooler removes 72.8 % <!-- R:K051 --> of the air-conditioning benefit globally, exceeds air conditioning in dry climates and contributes little in humid ones. Pairing a fan with air conditioning and raising the threshold by 3 K avoids 34 % <!-- R:K004 --> of air-conditioning operating hours at no exposure cost inside the fan window. These are exposure reductions on the SET scale for a seated, lightly clothed adult at outdoor conditions in a single year, not estimates of mortality or of indoor temperature. The lookup tables and window rules are released so that fan-first and low-energy cooling policies can be evaluated with the machinery that models already use for air conditioning.
