# Abstract

<!-- C01 -->
Heat-health and adaptation models represent cooling almost exclusively as air conditioning, written as a rule that operates above a threshold temperature and holds a setpoint. Electric fans and direct evaporative coolers, the most widespread and affordable cooling devices in hot regions, have no such representation and cannot be priced by the same exposure–response function. We write both technologies in the air-conditioning form, namely an equivalent temperature reduction ΔT applied inside an operating window. ΔT is the still-air dry-bulb reduction at outdoor humidity that gives the same Standard Effective Temperature as the intervention state, and windows follow published fan safety envelopes and the supply humidity of evaporative cooling. We tabulate ΔT on a temperature by humidity grid and apply it hour by hour to ERA5 2020 over global land and in 12 cities. Under the Default rule (23 °C, 55 % RH), a 1.2 m/s fan provides a mean equivalent temperature reduction of 3.6 K <!-- R:K001 --> and its window covers 96 % <!-- R:K002 --> of the hours in which air conditioning would operate, removing 61 % <!-- R:K003 --> of the exposure degree-hours that air conditioning removes. Raising the air-conditioning threshold by 3 K when a fan runs avoids 34 % <!-- R:K004 --> of air-conditioning operating hours with no loss of exposure benefit inside the fan window. Direct evaporative coolers reach an equivalence ratio of 72.8 % <!-- R:K051 --> globally, 112 % <!-- R:K054 --> in dry climates and 9 % <!-- R:K055 --> in humid climates. Any model that contains the air-conditioning rule can add both technologies through two lookup tables and two window rules.
